# Causality — General Causality and How the System Works

## The Game

**Causality is a cooperative causal-investigation tabletop role-playing game for 2 to 5 players and one Game Master.**

Players portray causal investigators, the **Investigators**. They use **the System** to explore the causes of a mystery, act in alternate realities, and try to integrate their interventions into a coherent present.

This document defines the game's fictional foundation. The general causality theorem, its scientific applications, and the technology described below belong to this setting.

## Time Does Not Exist

> **Time does not exist. We perceive chains of causality: a coherent sequence of events, each a consequence of those before it.**

The **Now** is the currently observable state of the world, produced by this sequence. What the characters call the past is the set of causes that made it possible.

A coherent chain can be unwound, then rerun from an earlier state. The guiding image is reversible flow in a viscous fluid: an apparently mixed transformation retains a structure that reversing the motion can reveal.

## The Discovery of General Causality

In Causality's setting, advanced research unified quantum mechanics and the description of spacetime by replacing the temporal component with the **general causality theorem**.

This general theory describes relationships between events and the states they produce. Its applied research made the System possible: a device capable of reopening a causal sequence and rerunning it with new interventions.

## The System

**The System is a complex scientific device combining the capabilities of a quantum computer with the mass and energy properties of a micro black hole.**

It opens the **Causality Gate**, centered on the **Main Timeline**, and allows Investigators to rerun past causes within alternate-reality **Branches**.

The System maintains access to possible states, displays the known causal map, and compares the consequences of interventions with the facts required by the present. Investigator rings provide control, communication, and tracking interfaces.

## Causality Gate, Time Flow, and Branch

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **System** | Scientific device that makes causal exploration possible. “The System” is its name in the story. |
| **Causality Gate** | Opening created and maintained by the System, centered on the Main Timeline, providing access to causal replay. |
| **Time Flow** | Causal structure accessible through the Gate: the Main Timeline, earlier states, and active Branches up to the Now. |
| **Main Timeline** | Shared observable reality into which interventions must be integrated. |
| **Branch** | Short name for a **Branched Timeline**: an alternate reality in which an Investigator can act on causal chains. |
| **Merge** | Integration of a Branch into the Main Timeline after replaying and checking its consequences up to the Now. |
| **Now** | Observable present state that Investigators seek to rejoin and preserve in a coherent form. |

The Gate is the opening; the Time Flow is the structure it makes accessible. **Branch** and the rules term **Branched Timeline** refer to the same game element.

The map retains the 20 earlier **Atomic Time Units**, from TU20 to TU1, and the Now at TU0. Introducing the Causality Gate adds no units or new game cost.

## Dice: Dark-Energy Capsules

In the fiction, **Rewind Dice represent dark-energy capsules used to operate the System**. Spending a Rewind Die consumes a capsule when attempting to open a Branch.

Each Investigator's six capsules are represented at the table by **d4, d6, d8, d10, d12, and d20**. Their dice continue to use the existing Rewind Percentage formula and outcome categories. A failed attempt also consumes the committed capsule.

The percentile d10 remains the tool for action rolls; dice rolled for damage remain resolution tools. Those rolls consume no capsule. Spending a die as a Rewind resource does not remove the physical die needed for other rolls.

When the available capsules run out, the System can no longer maintain the Causality Gate, and access to the Time Flow closes. The Main Timeline remains the only observable reality. Possible Branch maintenance costs remain an open design question; this description adds no per-turn expenditure.

## Acting in an Alternate Reality and Returning to the Now

1. The System opens the Causality Gate, centered on the Main Timeline.
2. An Investigator chooses an earlier state and commits a capsule represented by a Rewind Die.
3. If the opening succeeds, they act in a Branch: observe, investigate, preserve evidence, or modify a causal chain.
4. They attempt a Merge onto the Main Timeline to return to the shared Now.
5. The System replays the consequences and checks that essential facts remain possible. Conflicts must be resolved to integrate the Branch.

An intervention must fit into a coherent present. Changing a cause can erase evidence, make an event impossible, or remove the conditions for the Investigators' existence.

## Cognitive Load and Unintegrated Realities

Experienced but unintegrated Branches remain in an Investigator's memory. These memories describe realities that differ from the shared world. Holding them increases their **cognitive load**, represented in the rules by **Mental Load**.

Non-Merged Branches and unresolved conflicts contribute according to the existing values: +30 per non-Merged Branch, +40 per major conflict, and +20 per minor conflict belonging to the character, plus other active penalties. At 100 or more, the Investigator falls into madness and loses coherence with the Now.

## Application to [Glass Fever Protocol](./scenarios/glass-fever-protocol-gm-prep.md)

Toulouse, 2142: the Investigators open the Causality Gate to explore the causes of the disease officially first recorded in 2117. The intervention area remains Toulouse, determined by the available energy. Branches must preserve the evidence needed for a cure and the causes that still make the sealed-city Now possible.

The scenario retains its mystery, starter and complete modes, conflicts, and resolution conditions.
