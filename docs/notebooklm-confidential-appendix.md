## 2. The Investigation's Hidden Engine

Doctor **Ilya Voss** is Glass Fever's true origin carrier. A former biological virology expert, he became the Investigators' physician during the crisis. In a clue brought back by an Investigator, he recognizes an early form of the virus resembling his own research.

To avoid identification by the organization, he creates a **Counter-System**, then explores TU20 to recover the pathogen's original structure. In the research archives he finds no parent strain or earlier historical chain. He understands that the disease comes from the future, very probably from himself.

While fleeing laboratory guards, he loses his **control ring**, confiscated as a quarantined fragment. Without this access, he cannot return to the Now.

Voss knows his family depends on the world produced by the epidemic. Without sealed cities, he never meets his wife; **Alice, one of the Investigators, is his daughter**, and is never born. The mission and access to the System also lose their coherent origin. Voss therefore chooses to reproduce the causes of the catastrophe.

At TU17, he joins a pulmonary study in Toulouse and takes a logistics role with access to sampling shipments. Using his expertise and research from the future, he creates the first Glass Fever strain in this medical chain.

**Proposed editorial reading:** the adversary protects a reality his family depends on, at the cost of a collective catastrophe. The revelation connects the biological mystery, the origin paradox, and an Investigator's existence. This reading explains the scenario's dramatic strength; it must remain confidential to preserve player discovery.

## 3. The False Culprit and Its Motivations

**L.C.L.B**, Les congénères de la liberation, neither created nor released the pathogen. The group genuinely conducts animal-liberation actions: a pet shop at TU18, then the R&D laboratory at TU17. It opens cages and wrecks equipment. Those acts justify investigation for trespass and vandalism; they do not prove responsibility for the disease.

Confusing activism, presence at the laboratory, and the disease's origin creates an official explanation. The trial and renewed media coverage reinforce the false causal chain.

The group centers on Malo Keravec, a vegetarian activist in his thirties seeking status through the movement; Ines Valcay, 25, an activist and former partner still attached to him; Lila Nouri, a younger student fascinated by his rhetoric; and Basile Ferran, a quiet student whose need to belong keeps him in the group. Ines helps distinguish their activism from pathogen handling.

## 5. Clue Chain and Cooperation

The prepared scenario's central reasoning is:

```text
Pulmonary study and logistics access
    → strain creation and circulation
    → first infection, then isolation of the area
    → clinical samples and cure evidence
    → global spread and sealed cities
    → institutions, Voss's family, and the Investigators' mission

Actual activist actions
    → judicial and media traces
    → incorrect public attribution of responsibility for the disease
```

This summary does not impose a sequence of scenes. Players may examine, correct, and replace causes as long as required dependencies remain explainable.

In the walkthroughs, Alice carries the medical-file and parentage stakes; Bob follows logistics and security; Charlie examines timestamps and Counter-System residue; Dana works on testimony and samples. Pathologist **Ren Arco** keeps the clinical sample useful for a cure. These functions illustrate cooperation without prescribing characters for every table.

## 6. Starter and Complete Modes

In **starter mode**, Voss is a hidden historical cause. The GM does not play his adversarial actions or Counter-System expenditures. Progress concerns clues, branches, conflicts, and samples.

The explicitly described table validation concerns **two players**, this mode without an active Time Offender, and a duration of **about four hours**. It reports that players discovered the story without an active enemy run by the GM.

In **complete mode**, Voss becomes an aware Time Offender. His Counter-System has a single-use set of d4, d6, d8, d10, d12, and d20. His knowledge progresses from unknown identities to alertness and then identified targets. He may reinforce false accusations, move ampoules, compromise evidence, or attract security. Interventions leave edited archives, inconsistent routes, broken seals, or watchlist entries.

The complete replay shows Bob creating replacement evidence to unblock Alice's branch. Alice has nevertheless reached Mental Load 100 and remains mentally lost in the described ending. The present and the truth can survive at the cost of a personal loss.

**Evidence limit:** these replays use forced values for teaching. They are not empirical validation of all mechanics or statistically representative outcomes.

## 7. Resolution Conditions

The preparation requires preservation of:

1. an explainable Glass Fever origin;
2. proof that Voss is the origin carrier;
3. the distinction between actual L.C.L.B vandalism and false responsibility for the pathogen;
4. cure evidence, or a coherent replacement, up to the Now;
5. the possibility of sealed cities and the present that authorized the mission;
6. the causal possibility of Alice's birth;
7. enough origin data to support cure research.

Preventing the epidemic completely without causal replacement may erase institutions, the mission, and Alice. Possible outcomes are complete convergence, partial resolution, psychological divergence, or causal rupture. A public presentation can announce different kinds of outcome without revealing their hidden conditions.

## 8. Editorial Review Points

These observations come from the French documents consulted. They identify review work without arbitrarily resolving it.

| Observation | Impact | Presentation-Source Choice |
|---|---|---|
| Section 7 of the French design document still gives an example of the present at unit 20, while its section 5.2, the quickstart, and Glass Fever place Now at TU0. | Possible confusion about map direction. | TU0 is Now; TU20 is the oldest earlier state. |
| Rewind categories are written “50-79%” and “21-49%,” although results can have decimals. | Bounds and rounding conventions need explicit editorial treatment. | Repeat the displayed categories without inventing a new rule. |
| The starter replay moves TU14 to TU8 using `14 - 6`, after a Rewind result of 11. The guide instead describes subtracting the Rewind Die result. | Apparent confusion between the consequence number and opening result. | Do not use that numerical example to demonstrate the consequence. |
| The complete replay includes a forced percentile 85, although the described die produces tens from 0 to 90. | Harmonize the example with the specified die. | Use only the values described for that die. |
| The complete replay uses d6 for a non-lethal baton, against d8 in the general table. | Clarify the example or justify an exception. | The presentation table follows the quickstart. |
| General complete convergence requires sufficient psychological continuity; the replay calls its ending complete convergence with Alice mentally lost. | Distinguish investigation resolution from character outcome. | Describe coherent reality that can leave lasting consequences. |
