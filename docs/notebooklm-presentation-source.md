## 3. Glass Fever Protocol: the Story That Opens the Presentation

### The World

The investigation's present, called the **Now**, is **2142**. Sealed cities survive the consequences of **[Glass Fever](scenarios/glass-fever-protocol-gm-prep.md)**, a disease that crystallizes lung tissue, officially first recorded in **Toulouse in 2117**, before becoming a global crisis.

The catastrophe shaped institutions, archives, and daily life. It is among the causes that make the present possible. The investigation therefore concerns both the truth about the disease and the coherence of the characters' world.

### The Initial Case File

Public archives accuse an animal-liberation activist group, **Les congénères de la liberation**, abbreviated **L.C.L.B**. Its documented actions include releasing animals and vandalizing a laboratory. The earliest available archives describe animals, damaged equipment, and a trial; the connection to the disease's origin remains to be examined.

Other pieces trouble the picture: impossible timestamps, contradictory shipment routes, and degraded medical records. The investigation begins with concrete traces to compare, witnesses to meet, and causes to uncover.

For a short trailer, simply refer to “a group accused by the archives” and reserve the name L.C.L.B for a longer presentation.

### The Players' Mission

The characters are the **Investigators**, using the **System** to reopen earlier causal states. Their mission is to:

1. understand the true origin of [Glass Fever](scenarios/glass-fever-protocol-gm-prep.md);
2. gather and preserve the evidence needed for a cure;
3. maintain a coherent present in which their mission and reality remain possible.

### Toulouse as the Investigation Area

In the French source mirrored here, **playable interventions are limited to Toulouse**. The System's spatial radius depends on available energy. Consequences of the crisis can arrive from elsewhere, but every cause to reopen, modify, or integrate must be within this Toulouse area.

This anchor gives the scenario a distinct identity: a city to read as an investigation scene, through archives, laboratory, clinic, logistical chains, and areas under sanitary lockdown. The source does not describe a tourist itinerary or detailed real-world locations to promise in the presentation.

### The Dilemma

Preventing a cause can erase evidence, make an institution impossible, or destroy the conditions of the investigation itself. Players must seek an actionable truth and a coherent outcome. An appealing solution can create a deeper problem in the causal chain.

**Proposed emotional promise:** discover together what really happened, then accept the cost of the version of the world you manage to preserve.

Source: [Glass Fever Protocol preparation, “Scenario Premise,” “Initial Player Briefing,” and “Main Timeline”](./scenarios/glass-fever-protocol-gm-prep.md).

## 5. Causality: the Game Proposition

**Established in the sources. Causality is a cooperative causal-investigation tabletop role-playing game for 2 to 5 players and one Game Master.** The GM prepares the hidden structure of a mystery. Players uncover facts through traces and explore alternate sequences in **Branches**, also called **Branched Timelines**.

### Time Does Not Exist

In this setting, we perceive chains of causality: a coherent sequence of events, each a consequence of those before it. The **Now** is the observable state of the world produced by these causes. Their sequence can be unwound and replayed, like reversible flow in a viscous fluid.

The visual analogy draws on [UNM Physics and Astronomy's Couette cell demonstration](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Couette_Cell_for_Displaying_the_Properties_of_Laminar_Flow_%282%29_-_UNM_Physics_and_Astronomy.webm). It illustrates reversibility; it does not prove the game's fictional science. The [setting foundation](./universe-and-system.md) contains the credit and license for this external reference.

### The System: Applying General Causality

**The System is a complex scientific device combining the capabilities of a quantum computer with the mass and energy properties of a micro black hole.** In the fiction, advanced research unified quantum mechanics and spacetime by replacing the temporal component with the **general causality theorem**. Applying this theory made the System possible.

### The Causality Gate and the Return to the Now

The System opens the **Causality Gate**, centered on the **Main Timeline**, the shared reality. This opening provides access to the **Time Flow**, the causal structure containing earlier states and active Branches.

Investigators replay past causes and act in alternate-reality Branches. They modify causal chains before attempting a **Merge** onto the Main Timeline to rejoin the shared **Now**. The future, whose causes do not yet exist, cannot be freely explored.

**Every intervention must be integrated into a coherent present.** Changing a cause can destroy evidence or erase the conditions for the Investigators' existence. Experienced but unintegrated Branches increase their cognitive load, represented by **Mental Load**, and can drive them into madness.

### Dark-Energy Capsules

In the fiction, the six **Rewind Dice** represent **dark-energy capsules used to operate the System**. Spending a die commits a capsule to an attempt to open a Branch. Exhausting this reserve prevents the Causality Gate from being maintained. Other action and damage rolls consume no capsule.

Characters have rings connected to the System. They communicate, consult known facts, and track branches, conflicts, energy, and Mental Load. In the fiction, the rings explain the shared view of the common board.

### The Three Levels of Investigation

| Level | Contents | Contribution to Play |
|---|---|---|
| GM's hidden structure | Facts, conditions, dependencies, evidence | Prepared logic that players can discover and test. |
| Main Timeline | Confirmed facts and the observable shared state | A common representation of what the team knows and has integrated. |
| Branched Timelines | Interventions and experienced alternate states | Investigation opportunities that still need to fit into the present. |

The map begins incomplete. Player hypotheses do not automatically become facts. Discovering evidence and merging develops shared knowledge.

Sources: [Universe and System](./universe-and-system.md), [Game Design, sections 1 to 6](./game-design.md), [Game Master Guide](./game-master-guide.md).

## 6. What Players Actually Do

### The Main Loop

1. Study clues and confirmed Main Timeline facts.
2. Choose an investigation question and a Time Unit to explore.
3. Spend a Rewind Die to attempt to open a Branched Timeline.
4. Act: meet someone, observe, recover, preserve, or modify a causal element.
5. Examine facts and evidence produced by the intervention.
6. Attempt a **Merge**, integrating the branch into the present.
7. Resolve contradictions, possibly through a corrective branch opened by another player.
8. Update the map and recalculate the active character's Mental Load.
9. Continue until the mystery is resolved or the Time Flow closes.

### Cooperation Through Causes

One player may discover evidence another needs. One branch may create a conflict another makes solvable. Scenario roles include medical records, logistics, System traces, and testimony. These are investigative functions in the examples, not a definitive list of character classes.

Tension comes from simultaneous decisions: which lead deserves another die, which evidence must be protected, which branch should be merged now, and who can bear another contradiction?

### Teaching Example Without Scenario Revelations

**Illustration invented for this presentation; not a required [Glass Fever Protocol](scenarios/glass-fever-protocol-gm-prep.md) scene.**

The team knows an archive room was officially closed after an alarm. An Investigator opens a branch to preserve a file there. Her intervention accidentally removes the alarm that was supposed to justify the closure. The evidence is recovered, but her branch now threatens a necessary fact.

Another Investigator creates a compatible replacement cause: a documented breakdown requires the same closure. If other dependencies remain coherent, the branch can merge and the file be preserved.

**Message:** finding evidence and making it compatible with reality are two steps in the same challenge.

Sources: [Quickstart](./quickstart.md), [Game Master Guide, conflict preparation and merge requirements](./game-master-guide.md).

## 7. Mechanics at Two Levels of Detail

For a teaser, retain three ideas: **dice represent dark-energy capsules; contradictions need repair; unintegrated realities weigh on the mind.** The details below support partner questions or a longer presentation.

### A Map of Twenty Earlier States

The Time Flow includes **20 earlier Time Units**, plus the **Now at TU0**. TU20 is the oldest prepared state; TU1 is closest to the present. Each Time Unit is **Atomic**: it cannot be subdivided to enter at an intermediate instant.

Scale depends on the scenario. In [Glass Fever Protocol](scenarios/glass-fever-protocol-gm-prep.md), reference periods cover events from 2116 to the Now of 2142; units are not equal fixed durations.

### Dice as Limited Energy

Each Investigator receives a classic seven-die set: d4, d6, d8, d10, percentile d10, d12, and d20. **Six** are Rewind Dice: d4, d6, d8, d10, d12, d20. Each represents a **dark-energy capsule** spent when attempting to open a Branch; it is not normally recovered while the Time Flow remains accessible through the Causality Gate. Action and damage rolls consume no capsule.

Any of these six dice may be spent for any distance from 1 to 20 units. Die size affects opening chances; it does not define an authorized range.

```text
Rewind Percentage = die result / distance from the Now × 100
```

| Outcome Described by the Rules | Effect |
|---|---|
| 80% or more | Critical success: successful opening. |
| 50 to 79% | Partial success: opening with a negative consequence. |
| 21 to 49% | Partial failure: no stable branch, but a small investigation gain. |
| 20% or less | Critical failure: no stable branch and no small gain. |

Results may exceed 100%. Example from the quickstart: targeting TU14 with a d12 and rolling 9 gives about 64%, an opening with a consequence. These percentages are **opening scores**, not directly a die's success probability.

Consequences can include pursuit, a wrong entry point, a trace, or a conflict. A partial-failure gain may reveal a detail, condition, fact location, or new lead. Existing reference tables allow players to read outcomes without recalculating them.

### Conflicts and the Merge

A Merge replays the causal chain toward the Now and checks created, removed, or modified facts, their conditions, and their evidence.

- **Minor Conflict:** local disagreement compatible with the investigation's essential structure. The player chooses which version to impose and makes an action roll. Success applies the choice; failure applies the opposite version.
- **Major Conflict:** contradiction making an essential fact impossible. The merge is blocked until repaired through causal action or a corrective branch. A simple roll cannot resolve it.

### Mental Load

Characters start with **Mental Load 0**. At the end of the player's turn, it is recalculated from their own causal burden:

This gauge measures cognitive load created by experienced but unintegrated Branches and unresolved conflicts.

```text
Mental Load = 30 × the character's non-Merged branches
            + 40 × the character's unresolved Major Conflicts
            + 20 × the character's unresolved Minor Conflicts
            + other active penalties
```

An open branch and one major conflict therefore give Mental Load 70. One additional minor conflict brings it to 90. At **100 or more**, the character loses coherence with the Now; the rules describe this as falling into madness.

Mental Load is subtracted from action rolls. These use **one percentile d10**, read `00, 10, 20… 90`, with `00 = 0`. The final result must reach the required threshold. General quickstart thresholds are 0, 20, 50, 70, 90, and 100, from very easy to impossible.

Branches remain drawn. Even unintegrated, they were experienced by the characters and feed memories incompatible with the shared world.

### Violence Resolved Quickly

Investigators have **10 Health** and human limits by default. An attack accepted by the fiction hits automatically: only damage is rolled. Base categories are d4 for bare hands, d6 for an improvised object, d8 for a blade or non-lethal weapon, and d10 for a lethal weapon. At 0 Health, the target leaves the scene according to the fiction.

### Glass Fever's Specific Health Risk

Scenes exposing characters to the disease call for contamination tests. Contamination becomes a personal conflict and can cause damage during significant actions. Returning contaminated to a state before the disease exists can introduce an anachronistic infection and create a major conflict.

**Presentation value:** the disease connects setting, physical risk, and causal logic. This mechanic is a fictional scenario rule.

### Adversaries Within a Visible Logic

Advanced mode can introduce a **Time Offender**, a GM-controlled adversary using a **Counter-System**. They spend resources and act on facts, evidence, and branches within the specified limits. They are not automatically omniscient. [Glass Fever](scenarios/glass-fever-protocol-gm-prep.md)'s starter mode can be played without active pressure from such an adversary.

Sources: [Quickstart](./quickstart.md), [Design Notes](./design-notes.md), [Investigator Sheet](./investigator-sheet.md), [dice reference tables](./abacus/README.md), [scenario, “Contamination Risk”](./scenarios/glass-fever-protocol-gm-prep.md).
